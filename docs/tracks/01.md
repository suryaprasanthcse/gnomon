# Track 01 · Agents that reason over a graph and act on it

GNOMON enters Track 01. Its agents do not read the graph and guess: they walk it with graph algorithms exposed as MCP
tools, price each action in a copy of the world, and commit only the winner, with the path that justified it attached.
Sections refer to `docs/design/master.md`; proofs are the rows of its section 26, run by `scripts/demo_proofs.sh`.

| Focus area | How GNOMON meets it | Where | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Multi-hop reasoning | `Exec.blastRadius` walks `DEP_NOW` from a failing service and weights every service and team reached (sec 15.6); the federated version (scorer v2) also counts consumers in other tenants | `gnomon/executive/`, `gnomon/federation/` | Proof A, Proof E |
| Graph queries and algorithms as agent tools | `neighborhood`, `rank_critical` (`algo.pageRank`), `brokers` (`algo.betweenness`), `failure_domains` (`algo.WCC`), `communities` (`algo.labelPropagation`), `dependency_path` (`algo.SPpaths`) run on a projection graph in FalkorDB (sec 10.5) | `gnomon/query/algorithms/`, `gnomon/mcp/tools.py` | Proof F |
| Tool selection through MCP | 20 tools, offered per agent by what its layers and role allow; every tool call is logged and a decision cites it as `rationale_ref` (sec 20) | `gnomon/mcp/server.py` | Proof B |
| Taking actions based on what the graph shows | Rules R12 to R15 and R18 (`IsolateService`, `FailoverService`, `RollbackDeploy`, `EscalateIncident`, `RestoreService`) can be committed only through a decision that simulated them first | `gnomon/rules/titan/` | Proof A, Proof B |
| Explainable results | A `Decision` keeps every `Candidate` with integer `benefit`, `harm` and `score_num`, the `Evidence` paths behind them and the `APPROVES` edge of the human who signed; `why` walks it back; audit K7 re-simulates and compares | `gnomon/audit/k7.py`, MCP `why` | Proof A, Proof B, Proof G |

**What a judge can run.** `scripts/demo_proofs.sh A B E F` (or `scripts/demo_proofs.ps1 A B E F`) replays these four
proofs against the compose stack. Proof A shows three branches side by side with their integer scores while the live
`Head.hash` does not move. Proof B shows a human veto forcing re-selection, and the `APPROVES` edge appearing only on
the committed decision. Proof E shows the platform choosing failover because isolating would harm two other tenants.
Proof F asks the log what it would have done at an earlier sequence number.

**Known limit most relevant here.** Simulation fidelity is model fidelity (sec 28, item 1): action rules write the
expected state, and the truth of the synthetic world arrives later through `Observe.health`.

### Q1. Walk the integer arithmetic for one candidate in proof A.

Answer: Proof A's world is c (criticality 2, down), b (3) and a (3) in a hard chain a depends on b depends on c, plus f (4) depending softly on c. Each impacted node weighs criticality x severity x (2520 / (level + 1)), with severity 2 for down and 1 for degraded: c 2*2*2520 = 10080, b 3*2*1260 = 7560, a 3*2*840 = 5040, f 4*1*1260 = 5040, so B = 27720. For the candidate FailoverService to c-r1, the branch has c healthy and nothing impacted: benefit = 27720, harm = 0 (no new team), teams_new = 0, and score_num = benefit * LAMBDA_D - LAMBDA_N * harm = 27720 * 2 - 3 * 0 = 55440. IsolateService on c only removes the soft dependent f: benefit 5040, score_num 10080. EscalateIncident (k = 0) scores 0. 55440 is at least MARGIN * LAMBDA_D = 2520, so the failover wins.

### Q2. Why are P1 and E0 absent in sim mode?

Answer: Sim mode applies a rule body to a disposable branch to see what the world would look like, before any decision exists. P1 checks a decision envelope (scorer, sha, approval, cites) and E0 records the Decision, Candidates and Evidence, so there is nothing for P1 to check and nothing worth recording: the composer emits P0, BODY, Q0 and RET only, bind needs no attach, and diagnosis skips the decision check. The branch is deleted afterwards and live is never touched.

### Q3. What makes a decision stale, and why is that the right behaviour?

Answer: A decision is stale when a view key it depends on was written after its read_seq, here the enablement keys of its candidates plus the footprint keys of every service in the blast region, the incident and its root cause. The commit then returns REJECTED_STALE (P0, D5), the Executive flushes its branches, takes a new snapshot with attempt + 1 and decides again. That is right because the scores were computed on a world that has since changed in a way the decision relied on, for example an ownership retraction inside the region; committing it would act on a stale picture, while a change outside the footprint does not block it.

### Q4. How does K7 rebuild the base?

Answer: K7 replays the log up to the decision's read_seq into a fresh graph (the same replay engine K4 uses) and verifies it is gapless and linked. It checks the scorer library hash against the recorded scorer_sha and that a Scorer with that sha was active at the commit, and the configuration constants. It then rebuilds the rule set from that graph's Rule nodes, copies the base once per recorded candidate, simulates it in sim mode, scores it with the library and compares outcome, reason and benefit, harm, teams_new and score_num exactly. It also recomputes the winner, the footprint and its digest, and the evidence and cites, and deletes every graph it built.

### Q5. Defend λ = 3/2.

Answer: Lambda = LAMBDA_N / LAMBDA_D = 3/2 means one unit of harm costs 1.5 units of benefit, so the score is risk-averse: an action that removes 10 units of impact but adds 8 scores 10 * 2 - 3 * 8 = -4 and loses to doing nothing, while an action that adds nothing needs only a positive benefit. The ratio is a policy choice, not derived; it keeps both numbers small integers, so score_num = benefit * 2 - 3 * harm is exact with no division, and a change of lambda shows up in K7 as a configuration mismatch.
